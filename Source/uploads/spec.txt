18	1. Executive Summary
19	Our client operates a China-to-India cross-border wholesale and freight business — functionally similar to Alibaba, but positioned as a sourcing and fulfilment intermediary for Indian businesses. The client either sells Chinese products as a wholesaler (handling the full landed cost) or provides freight-only services for goods the buyer has already purchased from marketplaces such as Alibaba. Today this operation runs on WhatsApp chats, phone calls, spreadsheets, and PDF catalogs exchanged manually. As deal volume, vendor count, and product breadth grow, that manual approach is becoming the primary constraint on the business.
20	This document specifies an internal operations platform that gives the business owner and her staff a single system to capture leads, manage customers and vendors, maintain product catalogs with vendor PDFs, track inventory and outbound shipments, dispatch catalogs to customers over WhatsApp in one click, and see the health of the business through dashboards. It is scoped deliberately: Phase 1 is an internal tool (no customer or vendor logins), commerce/finance is intentionally lightweight (a simple invoice per deal, without full quote-to-cash automation), and WhatsApp uses a click-to-chat deep link rather than the paid Business API. Customer-facing portals, quote-to-cash, and API-based messaging are defined here as later phases so the platform can grow without rework.
21	The remainder of the document establishes the business context and model, the users and their roles, the core business use cases, detailed functional requirements by module, a proposed data model, non-functional requirements, a phased delivery roadmap, and a register of open questions and assumptions for the client to confirm.
22	2. Business Context
23	2.1 The Business
24	The business is a cross-continent trading intermediary that connects Chinese manufacturers and wholesalers with Indian buyers (retailers, resellers, and small-to-mid businesses). Buyers reach the business informally today — through WhatsApp messages and phone calls — asking for products, prices, and catalogs. The business sources from a roster of Chinese vendors, arranges freight and, where required, insurance and customs handling, and delivers goods into India. Revenue comes from wholesale margin (on fully-sourced deals) and from freight/logistics service fees (on freight-only deals).
25	2.2 Business Model — Two Service Types
26	A defining characteristic of this business is that it serves two distinct customer types, and the platform must model both first-class. The difference is who owns the cost and insurance legs of the transaction:
27	[TABLE]Dimension | CIF customer (full-service) | Freight-only customer
28	What the business handles | Cost + Insurance + Freight. Sources the product from a Chinese vendor and delivers landed goods. | Freight only. Moves goods the customer already bought elsewhere.
29	Where the customer buys | From our client (client is the seller/wholesaler). | Directly from Alibaba or another brand/marketplace.
30	Cost & insurance owned by | The business (client). | The external brand / marketplace (e.g. Alibaba).
31	Vendor relationship | Client's own vendor roster is used. | Vendor is external; client may only need pickup/consolidation details.
32	Revenue model | Product margin + service/logistics fees. | Freight & logistics service fee.
33	Data the platform must hold | Product, vendor, pricing, insurance, freight, landed cost. | Shipment/freight details, reference to external purchase, freight fee.
34	[/TABLE]
35	
36	Terminology note: CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight) is an Incoterm where the seller bears cost, marine insurance, and freight to the destination port. In this business it is used more loosely to mean the full-service tier. Freight-only customers are effectively an FOB-style arrangement from the customer's perspective — they own the goods at origin and only buy transport. The platform should treat “service type” as a core attribute of every customer and every deal, because it changes which fields, documents, and workflow steps apply.
37	2.3 Product Categories
38	Goods handled today span six categories. Each carries different handling, compliance, and catalog characteristics, so category is a structural attribute used across vendors, catalogs, inventory, and analytics:
39	[TABLE]Category | Typical handling / compliance considerations
40	Jewellery | High value-to-weight; hallmarking/quality claims; theft-sensitive; may attract higher duty and insurance.
41	Furniture | Bulky/volumetric freight; often FCL or LCL; assembly and damage-in-transit risk.
42	Stationery | Low unit value, high volume; commoditised; margin sensitive to freight cost.
43	Handbags | Brand/IP and counterfeit sensitivity; quality grading; moderate value density.
44	Electronics | BIS/compliance and battery (dangerous-goods) considerations; warranty and DOA handling; higher duty.
45	Tools | Weight-heavy; possible dual-use/HS-code sensitivity; durability and quality grading.
46	[/TABLE]
47	
48	The category list must be configurable (add/rename/retire) rather than hard-coded, because the client expects the product mix to change over time.
49	2.4 Problems the Platform Solves
50	Lead leakage: enquiries arrive over WhatsApp and calls with no structured capture, so leads are forgotten and follow-ups are missed.
51	Fragmented records: customers, vendors, and product details live across chats, notebooks, and spreadsheets with no single source of truth.
52	Manual catalog sharing: sending the right vendor catalog PDF to the right customer is a repetitive, error-prone manual task.
53	No inventory visibility: what is on hand, what has shipped, and what has left the firm's inventory is not tracked systematically.
54	No analytics: the owner cannot see lead volume, conversion, category mix, CIF-vs-freight split, or vendor performance to make decisions.
55	Key-person dependency: knowledge sits in the owner's head and chat history, making it hard to delegate to staff wearing multiple hats.
56	3. Scope
57	3.1 In Scope — Phase 1
58	Phase 1 delivers an internal operations tool used only by the business owner and her staff. There are no external logins. The following capabilities are in scope:
59	Customer intake: a structured intake form/page to capture leads that arrive via WhatsApp or phone, including service type (CIF vs freight-only).
60	Customer management (CRM): view, search, segment, and track customers and their enquiry/deal history.
61	Vendor management: onboard and maintain vendors mapped to one or more product categories.
62	Catalog management: catalog sections/listings per vendor, with one or more vendor PDF documents attached per catalog section.
63	Inventory & shipment tracking: track on-hand inventory, record outbound shipments, and reflect stock leaving the firm's inventory.
64	Invoicing: generate a simple, document-based invoice per CIF deal or freight-only service, track its status, and share the invoice PDF with the customer (lightweight — not full quote-to-cash).
65	WhatsApp catalog dispatch: a one-click button that opens WhatsApp pre-addressed to the selected customer so the owner attaches and sends the vendor PDF.
66	Analytics & dashboards: operational dashboards covering leads, customers, categories, CIF-vs-freight split, vendors, and inventory.
67	Roles & access: multiple internal roles so staff wearing several hats have appropriate access.
68	3.2 Out of Scope (Deferred to Later Phases)
69	[TABLE]Deferred capability | Rationale / target phase
70	Customer-facing portal (logins, self-service order/shipment views, self-service catalog requests) | Confirmed in scope for the business but not yet — targeted for Phase 2/3.
71	Vendor self-service portal (vendors uploading their own catalogs/PDFs) | Later phase; Phase 1 staff upload vendor PDFs on their behalf.
72	Full quote-to-cash: RFQ/quotations, orders, payment gateway/reconciliation, landed-cost engine | Commerce remains deliberately lightweight in Phase 1; a simple document-based invoice is now in scope (Section 6.8), but RFQ, orders, payment processing/reconciliation, and the landed-cost engine are Phase 2.
73	WhatsApp Business API (automated PDF send, templates, delivery receipts, inbound capture) | Phase 1 uses click-to-chat deep link; API is a Phase 2 upgrade path.
74	Accounting / GST / customs-broker integrations | Later phase; assume external tools handle finance and compliance for now.
75	Native mobile apps | Web-responsive is sufficient for Phase 1.
76	[/TABLE]
77	3.3 Constraints & Guiding Principles
78	Single-tenant internal tool for one business; multi-tenant SaaS is not a Phase 1 requirement.
79	Configurable master data (categories, service types, statuses) instead of hard-coded lists, so the business can evolve without code changes.
80	Design the data model now for customers, vendors, catalogs, shipments and deals so that Phase 2 commerce and portals bolt on without rework.
81	Mobile-responsive web UI, since staff will use phones alongside WhatsApp.
82	4. Users & Personas
83	In Phase 1 all users are internal. Because the team is small and staff wear multiple hats, roles are defined by capability rather than assuming one person per role — a single staff member may hold several roles.
84	[TABLE]Persona / Role | Description | Primary responsibilities in the platform
85	Business Owner (Admin) | Owner of the business; ultimate decision-maker. | Full access; configures master data and roles; monitors dashboards; dispatches catalogs; oversees vendors and customers.
86	Sales / Customer Executive | Handles enquiries from WhatsApp and calls; nurtures customers. | Creates and works leads via intake; manages customer records; sends catalog PDFs over WhatsApp; updates deal status.
87	Vendor / Sourcing Manager | Manages the Chinese vendor roster and catalogs. | Onboards vendors; maintains catalog sections; uploads and versions vendor PDFs; maps vendors to categories.
88	Inventory / Logistics Executive | Manages stock and outbound shipments. | Records inbound stock; tracks on-hand inventory; records shipments leaving inventory; updates shipment status.
89	Viewer / Analyst (optional) | Read-only stakeholder (e.g. partner/accountant). | Views dashboards and records without edit rights.
90	[/TABLE]
91	
92	Access model: Role-Based Access Control (RBAC). Roles are additive — the platform assigns one or more roles to a user, and the effective permission set is the union. The Owner/Admin role is a superset. Every create/update action is attributed to a user and time-stamped for accountability.

160	6.5 WhatsApp Catalog Dispatch (Click-to-Chat)
161	Removes the manual friction of sending a catalog. Phase 1 uses a WhatsApp click-to-chat deep link — no paid Business API. The platform pre-addresses the chat and pre-fills a message; the staff member attaches the PDF and hits send.
162	[TABLE]ID | Functional Requirement | Priority
163	FR-WA-01 | On a customer record (and on a catalog view listing customers), provide a "Send via WhatsApp" button. | M
164	FR-WA-02 | The button opens WhatsApp (wa.me / WhatsApp deep link) pre-addressed to the customer's stored number, with a pre-filled, editable message referencing the selected vendor/catalog. | M
165	FR-WA-03 | Make the selected catalog PDF readily available at the same moment (download/open) so the user can attach it in WhatsApp with minimal steps. | M
166	FR-WA-04 | Log each dispatch against the customer: which catalog/PDF, which staff member, and timestamp. | M
167	FR-WA-05 | Provide a configurable message template (with placeholders like customer name / catalog name) for the pre-filled text. | S
168	FR-WA-06 | From a catalog section, allow selecting a customer from the list and launching the WhatsApp dispatch in one flow. | S
169	FR-WA-07 | Design the dispatch module so it can later be upgraded to WhatsApp Business API for fully automated PDF sending and delivery receipts (Phase 2). | S
170	[/TABLE]
171	
172	Technical note: The click-to-chat deep link cannot itself attach a file — WhatsApp's URL scheme supports pre-filled text and a target number only. Phase 1 therefore optimises the two-step flow (open chat + one-tap attach the already-open PDF). True automated PDF delivery requires the WhatsApp Business API and is captured as a Phase 2 upgrade (FR-WA-07). This limitation is called out in the assumptions register.

205	7. Conceptual Data Model
206	The following core entities and relationships underpin Phase 1 and are designed to extend cleanly into Phase 2 commerce and portals. This is a conceptual model, not a physical schema.
207	[TABLE]Entity | Key attributes | Key relationships
208	Customer | id, name, business name, phone/WhatsApp, email, region, source channel, service type (CIF / freight-only), status, owner (staff), tags, timestamps | Has many Interactions, Dispatches, (later) Deals & Shipments; assigned to one Staff owner
209	Interaction / Note | id, customer_id, author, type, text, timestamp, follow-up date | Belongs to a Customer; created by a User
210	Vendor | id, name, contact, phone, region, status, commercial terms (MOQ, lead time) | Mapped to many Categories; has many Catalog Sections
211	Category | id, name, active flag | Linked to Vendors, Catalog Sections, Customers (interest), Inventory Items
212	Catalog Section | id, vendor_id, title, category_id, tags, timestamps | Belongs to a Vendor; has many Catalog Documents (PDFs)
213	Catalog Document (PDF) | id, catalog_section_id, file ref, filename, version, size, uploaded_by, uploaded_at | Belongs to a Catalog Section; referenced by Dispatches
214	Dispatch (WhatsApp log) | id, customer_id, catalog_document_id, staff_id, message, timestamp | Links a Customer and a Catalog Document; created by a User
215	Inventory Item | id, name, category_id, vendor_id, unit, on-hand qty, reorder threshold | Belongs to Category/Vendor; referenced by Shipment Lines
216	Invoice | id, customer_id, shipment_id (nullable), invoice_number, date, amount, tax, currency, status, pdf file ref, created_by, timestamps | Belongs to a Customer; optionally references a Shipment; PDF may be referenced by a Dispatch when sent via WhatsApp
217	Shipment | id, customer_id, destination, dispatch date, status, service type, reference docs | Belongs to a Customer; has many Shipment Lines
218	Shipment Line | id, shipment_id, inventory_item_id, quantity | Links a Shipment to an Inventory Item
219	User / Staff | id, name, email, roles[], active | Owns Customers; authors Interactions & Dispatches
220	[/TABLE]
221	
222	Deliberate design choices for future-proofing: service type is modelled on both Customer and Shipment; the Dispatch entity exists now (even for click-to-chat) so that Phase 2 Business-API sends slot into the same log; and Shipment/Shipment Line anticipate the Phase 2 Order/Deal entity without requiring it in Phase 1.

270	CIF | Cost, Insurance & Freight — here, the full-service tier where the business handles cost, insurance and freight and sells landed goods.
271	Freight-only | The business moves goods the customer already bought elsewhere (e.g. Alibaba); cost and insurance sit with the external brand.
272	FOB | Free On Board — buyer takes ownership at origin port; conceptually close to the freight-only customer's position.
273	Click-to-chat | WhatsApp deep link (wa.me) that opens a chat pre-addressed to a number with pre-filled text; no API required.
274	WhatsApp Business API | Meta's programmatic messaging API enabling automated sending of files, templates and delivery receipts (Phase 2).
275	RBAC | Role-Based Access Control — permissions granted via roles assigned to users.
276	MOQ | Minimum Order Quantity a vendor requires.
277	Invoice (Phase 1) | A lightweight, document-based bill generated per deal; status-tracked manually, without a GST tax engine or payment gateway integration (Phase 2).
278	Landed cost | Total cost of a product delivered to destination incl. product, freight, insurance, duties and fees.
279	MoSCoW | Prioritisation: Must / Should / Could / Won't-have.
280	FSD | Functional Specification Document (this document).
